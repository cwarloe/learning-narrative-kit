"And it knows how heavy it is, and that it can't ship by ground to Alaska. All of that is bundled inside it." That was encapsulation, and it was why the vendor could add photos and spec sheets to every product without touching anything else. It was also why Dana's SQL couldn't reach inside. The ETL would have to go through the vendor's export, which ran at two in the morning, so web orders would reach the warehouse a day late. Marcy said a day late was fine for fencing and not fine for chicks, and they left it there.

The second problem came from Republic, which lost its internet twice in March and couldn't look up a loyalty card either time. Teo wanted a distributed database, with the data stored on servers at the stores so each one could keep working on its own and sync later. He walked Dana through the choices on the same whiteboard. Full replication put a copy of everything at every store: simple, and a lot of disk for a store that would never look up a customer from Sandpoint. Fragmentation split the tables so the Colville server held only Colville's rows, which meant Colville couldn't find Gordon if he'd signed up in Kettle Falls.

"Gordon shops in both," Dana said.

"That's why I want allocation." Each store would keep its own customers and transactions, plus a copy of the product catalog it used all day. It was the right design. It also meant a server in every store, and when Walt saw that line he put it in the same column as the full warehouse: next fiscal year.

Hank's contribution was one sentence. The warehouse vendor's trial now held every customer's name, address, and phone number, and he wanted data encryption on all of it, in transit and at rest, so that anyone who got a copy would get ciphertext. The vendor charged extra for it. Hank paid.

The merge ran on a Saturday. It collapsed 1,940 duplicate cards into 811 customers, including Gordon, who went from four cards to one and was mailed a single apology letter with one coupon.

It also merged Dale Hollister Sr. and Dale Hollister Jr., who ran different operations from the same mailing address and did not speak to each other. Dale Jr. found out when his points balance doubled. Splitting them back apart took Dana most of a day, because the data model had no way to say "same address, different customer," and she had to add a field to do it.

On Monday the vendor who had pitched a graph database called back to follow up. Dana listened differently this time. In a graph, customers were nodes and the relationships between them were edges, so "same address" and "same customer" would have been two different kinds of line, and the merge would never have confused the Hollisters. It was the right tool for the problem she'd spent Sunday on. It cost more than the mailer. She thanked him and kept the field.

Walt approved the merchandising data mart and declined the full warehouse until next fiscal year. Marcy's mineral-feed test was still running. The spring mailer went out again to the corrected list, two weeks late, after most of the fencing season had started.
