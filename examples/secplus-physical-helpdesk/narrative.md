# Portland Desk — Badge, Lock, Eyes

*A Security+ physical security precursor • Hover over highlighted terms for exam definitions and supporting desk jargon.*

Monday mist sticks to bike lights like it has opinions. I am Ethan, still week-something at the [[help_desk|help desk]], and today the queue is not ports or passwords — it is doors, politeness, and the hardware that decides who gets to walk in.

Jules has a sticky under *which decision?* that says *did they badge, or did they smile?* Bike helmet on the monitor arm. She is not teaching fortress architecture. She is keeping one kind stranger from becoming a free ride into the building.

"[[physical_controls|Physical security controls]] are the parts you can stub your toe on," she says when I reach for a blank card. "Fences, locks, cameras, badges, habits. Digital policy fails open if the door fails polite. Your job is the manners — challenge the follow-in, lock the laptop, escalate the weird badge, and never treat a smile as authentication."

I open the queue. Coffee. Portland doing Portland. First [[ticket|ticket]] subject: *Visitor stuck at glass — also someone held the door for me yesterday and it felt weird after.*

## Maya and the door that loves strangers

Maya from accounting is in the [[lobby|lobby]] with a client and a face that apologizes for existing.

"I badged in," she says. "He was right behind me with a laptop bag and a confident wave. I held the door. Then I remembered the phishing week and felt like I had sponsored a heist."

I walk out. Glass vestibule. Two [[door_lock|door locks]] in sequence that only unlock one side at a time when the system is happy — our [[mantrap|mantrap]]. Today the outer leaf is propped with a rubber wedge because someone thought "airflow" was a security control.

"That wedge turns a mantrap into a courtesy hallway," I say. "You did [[piggybacking|piggybacking]] — he entered with your consent. If he had slipped in silently behind you without the wave, that is [[tailgating|tailgating]]. Either way: he did not authenticate. Politeness is not a factor."

Maya looks stricken. "So I…?"

"You stop, let the door close, and send him to [[reception|reception]]," I say. "Reception checks ID, writes the [[visitor_log|visitor log]], prints a guest [[access_badge|access badge]], and assigns an [[escort|escort]]. You do not improvise hospitality at the glass."

We pull the wedge. Facilities Slack gets a photo and a soft voice. Jules adds a sticky for the vestibule: *held doors are not badges.*

## Bollards, fencing, and the building that pretends to be boring

Mid-morning, a new hire asks why our street face looks like a quiet office and not a datacenter postcard. Network walks him past the loading dock with me tagging along because the ticket subject is *why can't trucks pull up to the glass?*

"[[bollards|Bollards]] keep enthusiastic vehicles from interviewing the lobby," the engineer says. "[[fencing|Fencing]] and the gate are the outer delay. Temporary [[barricade|barricades]] go up when the street fair gets ideas. [[lighting|Lighting]] on the lot is not ambiance — dark corners are where cameras go blind and people get creative."

The building itself is deliberately dull from the sidewalk — no giant logo, no "SERVERS INSIDE" sculpture. Jules calls it [[industrial_camouflage|industrial camouflage]] without romance. [[signage|Signage]] is sparse and specific: *Authorized personnel*, *All visitors check in*, not a tour brochure.

I write the desk line: perimeter is delay and deterrence; the ticket is usually about the door habit, not the steel.

## Cameras, motion, and the guard who can ask a question

Afternoon ticket from HR: someone left a backpack in the stairwell and wants footage "from the vibe of 2-ish."

[[facilities|Facilities]] pulls [[cctv|CCTV]] coverage for the stair landing. Retention is finite; angles are honest about what they miss. A [[motion_detection|motion detection]] alert on the roof hatch fired last week and turned out to be a seagull with management potential. The [[alarm_system|alarm system]] on the [[server_room|server room]] door still means "stop and call," not "curious peek."

A [[security_guard|security guard]] meets us at the landing, because cameras watch and humans challenge.

"I can ask who you are," she says. "The lens cannot."

We do not find a villain — we find a contractor who signed the visitor log and then got lost. Escort gap, not Hollywood. Ticket note: cameras for after; guards for during; motion and alarms for "something moved that should not." If the hatch alarm is weird after hours, that is a [[soc|SOC]] / Facilities hunt — not a help-desk curiosity walk.

## Badges, biometrics, and the reader that is not a suggestion

Dev from engineering — reimage graduate, Wi-Fi trauma alumnus — pings that his badge works the lobby but fails the wiring closet.

"I wave it like a magic wand," he says. "Sometimes I badge and hold for a friend. Sometimes I let two people in on one beep because the line is awkward."

"One beep, one person," I say at the [[badge_reader|badge reader]]. "Your [[access_badge|access badge]] is a [[proximity_card|proximity / RFID]] credential. Convenient. Also cloneable in the wrong hands if we treat it as the only factor. Closet policy is badge plus [[biometrics|biometrics]] on the fingerprint reader — something you have and something you are. Holding the door for a friend is piggybacking with better branding."

Dev practices the fingerprint until the reader stops judging him. I revoke the "shared beep" culture in the ticket comments like a person who has seen too many polite breaches.

Nora from ops drops a loaner laptop at the desk with a cable dangling like a confession.

"Travel kit," she says. "Hotel desk. I stepped away for ice."

"[[cable_lock|Cable lock]] next time," Jules says. "Full-disk encryption is necessary and insufficient when the whole chassis walks. Lock the hinge to the furniture like you mean it."

## Clean desk, privacy filter, shoulder surfing

Finance row after lunch looks like a paper museum. Payroll printouts. Sticky with a door PIN. Laptop open to a vendor portal while someone "just grabs coffee."

"[[clean_desk|Clean desk policy]]," I say, trying not to sound like a hall monitor. "Sensitive paper goes in the locked drawer or the shred bin — not a decorative stack. Lock the screen. That PIN sticky is a gift to [[shoulder_surfing|shoulder surfing]]."

I hand out two [[privacy_filter|privacy filters]] for people who sit near the aisle. Narrow viewing angle. Café eyes and curious neighbors get a dark rectangle instead of a bonus spreadsheet. Maya installs hers like armor. Sam asks if the filter makes him look paranoid. Jules says paranoia is just pattern recognition with better marketing.

## USB blockers, Faraday, air gap

Sam — contractor, USB historian — appears with a phone and a story about a free charge kiosk at the airport that "felt hungry."

"Use a [[usb_data_blocker|USB data blocker]]," I say. "Power only. No data pins. Public charge ports should not get a conversation with your contacts."

Meanwhile Security drops an evidence phone into a metallic pouch before it rides to forensics.

"[[faraday_cage|Faraday]] bag," the analyst says. "Kill the radios so remote wipe and beaconing get a time-out. Not theater — RF timeout in a foil lunch bag with a pedigree."

In the same breath they remind me why the ballot-tabulation laptop for a client demo sits on a table with no Ethernet and Wi-Fi disabled hard.

"[[air_gap|Air gap]]," Jules translates. "No cable, no Wi-Fi, no 'just this once' USB bridge to a helpful desktop. Isolation is a physical decision, not a sticker."

I add a wiki line about [[protected_distribution|protected cable distribution]] for the sensitive runs in the [[server_room|server room]] — conduits and monitoring so taps are not a casual afternoon project — and leave the deep electrical romance to Facilities.

## Secure areas, visitor logs, hot aisles

Late day tour for a vendor who needs to reseat a drive. Reception already has them in the [[visitor_log|visitor log]]. Guest badge. Escort is me, which feels like typecasting.

We badge into the [[secure_area|secure area]]. Camera wink. Fingerprint. The room is loud in a purposeful way — [[hot_aisle|hot aisle]] / cold aisle layout so humans walk the cold path and the exhaust stays honest. I do not pretend to be a mechanical engineer. I pretend to be a person who does not prop doors for airflow.

Vendor works. Escort stays. No unattended strangers among the racks. Ticket closes with "visited, logged, escorted, left" — the boring sentence that keeps audits calm.


## Cipher locks, clones, and the badge that went on vacation

Rita from marketing Slack-panics: her badge is "somewhere between the food carts and destiny." She is using a colleague's card "just for today" and wants me to unlock the marketing closet remotely like a video game.

"We do not share badges," I say. "We disable hers in the system, print a temporary at reception, and treat the missing [[proximity_card|proximity card]] as lost media — because a found badge plus a helpful door is an unauthorized person with manners."

Facilities confirms the card is deactivated before lunch ends. Rita collects a temp, photographs it into her password manager like a person who has learned, and promises not to loan plastic again. Jules mutters that badge cloning demos exist for a reason and that PIN-on-reader plus biometrics is how we keep a copied card from becoming a copied career.

A separate ticket wants the old mechanical cipher lock on the storage closet "because numbers feel safer than RFID." I do not dunk on numbers. I say the [[door_lock|lock]] is only as good as rotation and shoulder-surfing resistance — and a code written on the frame is a public key in the worst sense.

## Night walk: eyes, ears, and the sticky that survives

Before close, Facilities invites the desk on a five-minute perimeter walk so we stop inventing building mythology in tickets. Lot lights are even; one camera looks at the bike rack on purpose; the guard's path crosses the dark corner the seagulls prefer. I notice how little of this shows up in the queue until something fails — then everyone discovers they always cared about [[lighting|lighting]] and [[cctv|CCTV]].

Back at the cube I add one more sentence to the onboarding blurb for interns: physical security is layered. Outer delay, middle detection, inner authentication, human challenge. The help desk sits where politeness tries to short-circuit the stack. Our power move is refusing the short circuit without being cruel about it.

## The line that sticks

5:35. Queue quieter. Mist still opinionated. I rewrite the desk blurb the wiki will actually keep:

Challenge follow-ins — tailgating and piggybacking are door failures wearing manners. Reception, visitor log, badge, escort. Do not wedge the mantrap. Perimeter is bollards, fencing, lighting, boring camouflage, and signage that means something. Cameras and motion and alarms watch; guards ask. Badge plus biometrics for closets; one beep, one person; cable-lock the loaners. Clean desk and privacy filters beat shoulder surfing. USB data blockers for hungry kiosks; Faraday for evidence radios; air gap when the network must not exist. Secure areas are processes, not vibes.

Jules clips her bike lights on. "How many fortress acronyms did you memorize?"

"None," I say. "I collected a few decisions — badge versus smile, lock versus hope, escort versus 'they seem fine' — and a few shapes that keep showing up on the exam sticky: [[tailgating|tailgating]], [[mantrap|mantrap]], [[cctv|cameras]], [[access_badge|badge]], [[biometrics|biometrics]], [[clean_desk|clean desk]], [[air_gap|air gap]]."

She nods once. "Physical is the reminder that computers live in buildings with doors. Tomorrow the queue invents a new way to hold one open."

I shut the laptop. The [[lobby|lobby]] glass reflects the mist. Somewhere a badge reader beeps for a person who actually belongs. That, for Monday, is enough.
