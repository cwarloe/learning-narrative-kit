# ITM 310 Exam 2: Study Cards

Every key term the Exam 2 study guide and review deck point to, sorted into the four rubber bands and turned into cards: 185 terms, 137 cards. The cards come from Bidgoli Modules 3, 9, 4, 5 and 6, Knaflic Chapter 1, and the CRISP-DM and Power BI lectures. Definitions follow the course glossary and the class slides; nothing here is a rival definition.

Each band asks for a different kind of study, so each gets its own deck.

| Band | What goes in it | Card | How to study it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red | Terms you only need to recognize | 3x5 | Fast. Read the definition, say the term, flip. If you hesitate, the card goes back in. Short bursts, twice a day, in the last two days. |
| Green | The parts of a system and what each part does | 4x6 | Say every part out loud before you flip. Missing one part counts as a miss. |
| Yellow | Look-alikes you have to tell apart | 4x6 | Say the difference in one sentence. The exam's wrong answers are usually the other look-alike. |
| Blue | Order, and getting from a situation to the answer | 4x6 | Put the steps in order, or reason from the clue to the answer, then check. |

Every card works the same way: click the card or press Space to flip it, and use the arrow keys or the buttons to move. Shuffle mixes the deck. The sort sheet at the bottom lists every term with its band, which is a good start on your one-page note sheet.

```lnk-deck
band: green

tag: CRISP-DM & POWER BI

A: A primary key in one table and a matching foreign key in another (fields with the same range of values).
band: green
```

## Yellow cards: tell them apart

```lnk-deck
title: Yellow · look-alikes
meta: 39 cards · say the difference in one sentence
size: 4x6
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Data warehouse, data mart, or data lake? Tell them apart.
A: Warehouse: data from many sources, prepared for decision making. Mart: a smaller warehouse for one department or function. Lake: data kept in its original format, structured and unstructured.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Sentiment data pulled from Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram posts: structured or unstructured?
A: Unstructured.
why: Review deck slide 8. Structured data fits rows and columns; posts, photos, and video don't. That is what a data lake can hold and a relational table can't.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: OLTP or OLAP? Which one runs the cash register and which one slices sales by time, product, and location?
A: OLTP runs transactions in real time (point of sale, data entry), mostly internal data. OLAP analyzes many sources in multiple dimensions to produce BI.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Business intelligence vs business analytics: what's the difference?
A: BI gives historical, current, and predictive views of operations. BA uses data and statistical methods to give decision makers insight they can act on.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Descriptive, predictive, or prescriptive analytics: which one reviews past events and reports what happened in a given period?
A: Descriptive. Predictive estimates what is likely to happen; prescriptive recommends what to do.
why: Review deck slide 10. This list is from the Bidgoli chapter, not the course glossary: check it against your book.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Data-mining analysis vs text-mining analysis: what does each look for?
A: Data mining discovers patterns and relationships in data. Text mining analyzes large amounts of text to capture key concepts, trends, and hidden relationships.
why: Review deck slide 11: the discovery of patterns and relationships is data mining.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Primary key vs foreign key?
A: A primary key uniquely identifies each record in its own table. A foreign key is a field that matches another table's primary key, so the tables can be cross-referenced.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Physical view vs logical view of data?
A: Physical: how data is stored on and retrieved from storage media. Logical: how information appears to users and how they can organize and retrieve it.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Hierarchical, network, or relational model: how do records relate in each?
A: Hierarchical: a tree; every child has one parent. Network: a record can have many parents and children. Relational: two-dimensional tables of rows (records) and columns (fields).
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: Sequential, random, or indexed sequential (ISAM) file access: which reads records in any order, and which switches between the two?
A: Random access reads in any order (fast for a few records). Sequential reads in stored order. ISAM does both: random for a few records, sequential for many.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 3 · DATA & BI
Q: A distributed DBMS can split data three ways. Fragmentation, replication, allocation: tell them apart.
A: Fragmentation divides tables among locations (horizontal, vertical, mixed). Replication gives each site a full copy. Allocation combines both: each site stores the data it uses most.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 9 · AI
Q: Forward chaining vs backward chaining in an expert system?
A: Forward chaining runs a series of if-then-else condition pairs from the facts toward a conclusion. Backward chaining starts from the goal (the "then" part) and works back to find the solution.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 9 · AI
Q: Four types of intelligent agents. Which one tracks equipment and networks to predict a crash?
A: Monitoring and surveillance agents. The others: shopping and information agents (search the Web), personal agents (fill in forms, finish e-mail addresses), data-mining agents (find trends in a data warehouse).
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 9 · AI
Q: Machine learning, neural network, genetic algorithm, or fuzzy logic: which one mimics natural evolution with mutation, selection, and crossover?
A: A genetic algorithm. Machine learning: computers learn from experience without explicit programming. Neural networks: learn to recognize patterns (faces, spam). Fuzzy logic: degrees of membership instead of yes/no.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 9 · AI
Q: Artificial intelligence vs augmented intelligence: what's the difference in goal?
A: AI tries to simulate human thought and behavior. Augmented intelligence aims to complement decision makers, not replace them.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 4 · ETHICS & LAW
Q: Misinformation vs disinformation: what's the one difference?
A: Intent. Disinformation is knowingly distributed. Misinformation is distributed regardless of intent to mislead.
why: Fake news is a story or hoax made to deceive on purpose.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 4 · ETHICS & LAW
Q: Deepfake vs cheap fake?
A: A deepfake is a fake video or recording that looks and sounds just like a real event. A cheap fake is made with cheap, easy-to-get software and edited with tools like Photoshop.
band: yellow
---
tag: MOD 4 · ETHICS & LAW
Q: Cookies vs log files: which one is stored on your own computer?
A: Cookies: small text files with unique IDs, saved on the user's hard drive. Log files: kept by the Web server, recording what users do on the site.
why: Review deck slide 23: cookies invade privacy because they collect information the user did not voluntarily share.
band: yellow
```

## Before you trust a card

8 cards carry lists the course glossary doesn't spell out, such as the five parts of a DBMS or the four Rs of case-based reasoning. They come from the Bidgoli chapters and say so on the back. Check them against your book.

The review deck and the study guide disagree on the name of the third view icon (label C). The study guide's key says Data View; slide 47 says Model View, and slide 46 says Relationships View. The C card gives Model view and says why. Ask your instructor which answer the exam will accept.

The Knaflic definitions are working summaries of Chapter 1, because the study guide names the ideas without defining them. Read them against the chapter.

Review deck slide 20 dates the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act to 1988. The act is from 1998. No card asks for the year.
